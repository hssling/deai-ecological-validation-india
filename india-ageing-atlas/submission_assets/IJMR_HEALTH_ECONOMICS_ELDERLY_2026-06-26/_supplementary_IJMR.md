# Supplementary material

Catastrophic health spending, impoverishment and the unpaid-care economy among older adults in India.

## S1. Variable definitions

- **Out-of-pocket spending (household, annual):** household inpatient cost over the previous year plus household outpatient cost (doctors' fees, diagnostics and medicines obtained during treatment) over the previous 30 days, multiplied by 12. Deflated to constant 2017 rupees.
- **Capacity to pay:** non-food consumption, defined as total household consumption minus annual food expenditure (WHO method).
- **Catastrophic health expenditure (CHE):** out-of-pocket spending above 10 and above 25 per cent of total consumption (budget-share definitions), and above 40 per cent of capacity to pay (WHO definition).
- **Impoverishment:** the rise in the share of older adults whose per-capita consumption falls below the poverty line once per-capita out-of-pocket spending is subtracted. The poverty line (Rs 18,307 per capita per year, constant 2017 rupees) was anchored to the survey's own poverty measure.
- **Concentration and Erreygers indices:** computed for the binary CHE40 outcome, ranking households by per-capita consumption (positive = concentrated among the better-off).

## S2. Sensitivity: adults aged 45 yr and over (n=66,470)

The analysis was repeated on the full LASI Wave 1 sample of adults aged 45 yr and over. The pattern was unchanged; the burden was somewhat lower than at 60+, consistent with a rising burden with age.

## S4. Catastrophic spending by per-capita-consumption quintile

| Consumption quintile | n | CHE >40% capacity, % | CHE >10% consumption, % |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 (poorest) | 5761 | 17.0 | 24.6 |

| 3 | 6339 | 20.8 | 36.7 |
| 4 | 6619 | 21.6 | 39.8 |
| 5 (richest) | 7206 | 26.1 | 45.2 |

*On the relative capacity-to-pay measure, catastrophic spending rises with consumption because wealthier households spend more on private care. This is the opposite of the impoverishment result, where absolute poverty is concentrated among poorer and rural households.*

| Education (per year) | -0.010 | +0.276 | -0.0029 | -3.2 |

*A positive contribution pushes catastrophic spending towards better-off households; a negative contribution pushes it towards poorer households. Multimorbidity (concentrated among the better-off) is the main pro-rich force; rural residence is the main pro-poor force.*
