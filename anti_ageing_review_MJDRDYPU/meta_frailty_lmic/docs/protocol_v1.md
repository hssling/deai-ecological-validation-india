# Protocol v1

## Title

Community-deliverable interventions for physical frailty in older adults: a systematic review, component network meta-analysis, and implementation-readiness mapping for Indian primary care.

## Target Journal

Primary target: Indian Journal of Medical Research.

## Registration

Protocol registration is required before submission. Draft registration target: PROSPERO.

## Review Question

Among prefrail or frail older adults, which community-deliverable exercise, nutrition, digital, caregiver-supported, or multidomain interventions improve frailty status and physical function, and which components are most feasible for Indian/LMIC primary care?

## Eligibility

Include randomized or cluster-randomized trials enrolling adults aged >=60 years, or trials with mean age >=65 years, where participants were prefrail, frail, physically frail, sarcopenic/frail or functionally vulnerable.

Primary settings are community, home, outpatient or primary-care settings. Nursing-home studies will be extracted but analysed separately unless needed for network connectivity.

## Primary Outcomes

- Frailty reversal or frailty-status improvement
- Frailty score
- Gait speed
- Short Physical Performance Battery
- Grip strength

## Synthesis

Pairwise random-effects meta-analysis will be performed where outcomes are compatible. Component network meta-analysis will be attempted only if the network is connected, clinically coherent and includes enough studies per node. Otherwise, a no-NMA audit will be reported.

## Implementation-Readiness Mapping

Intervention classes will be scored for deliverability in Indian primary care using setting, personnel, equipment, dose clarity, adherence, safety and procurement burden.

## Scoping Update: 2026-05-18

The first full PubMed scoping run reported 3425 hits and downloaded 3418 records. Automated title/abstract screening identified a large candidate field; therefore, full-text screening will start with a prioritized primary queue of community/primary-care/home-based frailty RCT candidates. Sarcopenia-only records are retained as a secondary stratum and will not drive the primary frailty conclusion unless frailty or functional-vulnerability outcomes are extractable.

Second-pass triage identified 179 high-confidence extraction candidates. Of these, 95 had PMC open-access full texts that were fetched and machine-mined for methods, randomization, intervention, comparator, outcome and table snippets; 84 require publisher/library full-text access. Machine-mined snippets are extraction aids only and do not replace author verification, risk-of-bias assessment or numeric effect extraction.

NMA remains conditional and is not approved until full-text extraction proves a connected, clinically coherent network with compatible outcomes and acceptable transitivity.
